How we use the Pyramid of Learning

In the Pyramid of Learning, academic learning sits at the top. The central nervous system and the senses form the base. This is where our Sensory Class begins. Children explore touch, balance, and the position of their bodies as they play.

From movement to learning

Writing a letter involves more than holding a pencil. A child steadies their body, looks at the paper, and guides their hand. In class, a child might first practise those same skills by steadying a bowl with one hand and spooning rice with the other.

Preparing for school through play

A game also gives children chances to listen, wait for a turn, and try again when something spills. With a teacher close by, they can practise managing frustration and returning their attention to the task. We offer each child time to explore these skills at their own pace.

Wonder Class

Some children scoop up coloured rice with both hands straight away. Others watch for a while, then reach out with one finger. At Wonder Tree, there’s room for both. Children can start wherever they feel comfortable.

What happens in Sensory Class?

Touch and Feel
Children scoop, squeeze, and pour. As they spoon rice into a cup or press dough between their fingers, they explore textures and practise guiding their hands. At home, you can use words like “soft”, “rough”, and “sticky” to describe food together.
Gross Motor
On the balance path, children watch where they put their feet and work out how to steady themselves. A teacher stays close and adjusts the challenge to suit their age.
Creative Time
In Creative Time, children explore arts and crafts through colouring, making paper collages, and finger painting. These activities help them practise fine motor skills with their hands and fingers. Choosing colours and shapes encourages creativity, while following each step gives them a chance to practise focusing.

Why we take Sensory Class outdoors

Out on the lawn, a breeze sets the ribbons dancing. Children can follow one through the frame, feel the grass beneath their feet, or stretch out their arms to balance on a stepping stone. The garden gives them room to move and time to notice little things, like a leaf casting a shadow on their mat.

Outdoor play also gives children time in natural light. Sunlight on the skin helps the body make vitamin D, which supports normal immune function.

Choose a class for your child

3-month term

Regular Sensory Class

Children learn with their age group for three months. At the end of the term, a psychologist observes them during activities to give families a picture of how their child is developing in class.

Parents also receive a progress report at the end of the term. An additional discount is available for siblings who enrol.

4 sessions per month

Weekend Sensory Class

Classes meet once a week on Saturday or Sunday, depending on the group. Families can pay monthly or for three months at a time. Weekend classes do not include a psychologist’s observation or a progress report.

6-month term

Growing Class

Each class day combines sensory play with preschool activities such as completing simple worksheets, drawing, or making crafts. These daily routines help children practise following directions, taking turns, and doing things for themselves.

Sensory Class in English

Children hear and use English as they play. When the teacher tips rice into a cup, for example, they hear the word pour as they watch the action. Little by little, they try saying familiar words and using them with their teacher and friends.

Outing Class & Pop-Up Class

From time to time, children in our three-month Regular Sensory Class head out with their teachers to explore somewhere new. On selected occasions, we also open registration to the public through a Pop-Up Class. Families can sign up for that event on its own.

Little Drummer · Drumming for young children

Two taps, a pause, then two more. In Little Drummer, children listen to a short rhythm and take a turn playing it back. With their teacher, they explore the sounds of drums and cymbals, try alternating hands, and practise starting and stopping together.
